**1. Five Elements & Flavor Mappings**

* **Wood (Mộc):** Bitter, Sour. Associated with the Liver (Gan), Spring season, and Green color.
* **Fire (Hỏa):** Salty, Pungent (Spicy). Associated with the Heart (Tim), Summer season, and Red color.
* **Earth (Thổ):** Sweet, Sour. Associated with the Spleen (Tỳ), Late Summer season, and Yellow color.
* **Metal (Kim):** Bitter, Pungent (Spicy). Associated with the Lung (Phổi), Autumn season, and White color.
* **Water (Thủy):** Salty, Sweet. Associated with the Kidney (Thận), Winter season, and Black/Blue color.

**2. Yin-Yang (Ohsawa) Properties**

* **Yin (Âm):** Cold, dark, passive, receptive, soft, wet, heavy. Associated with cooling foods (fruits, vegetables, grains).
* **Yang (Dương):** Hot, bright, active, creative, hard, dry, light. Associated with warming foods (meats, spices, fermented foods).
* **Balance Principle:** A balanced diet requires a harmony between Yin and Yang properties. Specific ratios (e.g., 5:1) are cited in Ohsawa methodology.

**3. Categorization Logic**

* **Elemental Logic:** Food is classified based on the Five Elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) and their corresponding flavors and colors.
* **Physiological Logic:** Food is categorized by Yin (cooling/nourishing) or Yang (warming/stimulating) properties.
* **Organic Logic:** Elements correspond to specific Zang-Fu organs (Liver, Heart, Spleen, Lung, Kidney) and seasonal cycles (Spring, Summer, Late Summer, Autumn, Winter).
* **Flavor Interaction Logic:** Foods are analyzed for flavor combinations (e.g., Sweet + Sour) to determine if they create harmony or discord in the body.
